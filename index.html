Data and methods

Tracings. PTB-XL v1.0.3 (Wagner P, Strodthoff N, Bousseljot RD, Samek W, Schaeffter T. Sci Data 2020;7:154), via PhysioNet, CC BY 4.0. Recordings are shown at their original 500 Hz with a 0.67 Hz zero-phase high-pass and 150 Hz low-pass applied here. Labels are the dataset's SCP-ECG statements from cardiologist review; likelihoods shown as given. Only human-validated records without noise flags were used.

3D vector. Frank vectorcardiogram reconstructed from leads I, II and V1–V6 with the Kors regression matrix (Kors JA et al. Eur Heart J 1990;11:1083–92). Lead directions and the teaching model's lead synthesis use a forward matrix fitted to these records by least squares; it agrees closely with the published Dower matrix. A single dipole explains 80–99% of each lead's signal; the rest is non-dipolar, which only the real tracings show.

Heart model. Adult heart with great vessels from Z-Anatomy, built on BodyParts3D (© Database Center for Life Science), CC BY-SA 4.0; simplified to 60,000 triangles for the web and placed in anatomical orientation. The heart vector is drawn from the center of the ventricles.

Activation animation. The order in which muscle activates is a template from human isolated-heart mapping (Durrer D et al. Circulation 1970;41:899–912), with variants for bundle branch blocks, fascicular blocks, pre-excitation (left lateral pathway assumed), RV pacing and wide ectopic beats. Its timing is driven by the tracing itself: ventricles depolarize as this patient's QRS energy accumulates and recover across their T wave; atria fire on P waves detected from the record. It shows the sequence, not a patient-specific activation map.

Wiggers and PV loop. A lumped time-varying elastance model of both ventricles, left atrium, systemic and pulmonary circulations (Suga–Sagawa elastance; three-element Windkessel aorta), integrated at 0.5 ms. Ventricular contraction starts 40 ms after this patient's QRS onset and relaxation ends with their T wave; the atria contract on their detected P waves (none in AF or flutter). PTB-XL contains no pressures, so every pressure and volume shown is simulated; conditions are teaching presets, not this patient's physiology.

Guided read. Rate and axis are graded against the values shown on the printout; rhythm, hypertrophy and ischemia against the cardiologist statements. PTB-XL has no interval labels, so PR, QRS and QT are graded by class (short, normal, prolonged or wide) from the statements plus an estimate taken from a median beat of the reconstructed vector. That estimate is approximate and is only quoted when it agrees with the statements.

Not for clinical use. Teaching material. Interval measurements are left to the calipers on purpose.
